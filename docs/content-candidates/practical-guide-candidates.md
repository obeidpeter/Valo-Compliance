## GUIDE-C01 — Prepare an invoice from facts you can check

Status: DRAFT — EDP review and export checks pending.

An invoice is easier to understand when the customer can see what was supplied, how the amount was calculated and what to do next. Start with the agreed sale, rather than an old invoice whose details may belong to another customer.

Collect the seller's name, customer's name, document date, a unique reference and a clear description of the goods or work. Add quantities, units and agreed prices. If a payment date or payment instruction has been agreed, check it against the relevant message, quotation or order before including it. Leave uncertain information visibly incomplete.

For each line, calculate quantity multiplied by unit price. Then review any agreed discount, delivery charge or other adjustment. Check the final total separately from the wording. For example, a fictional sale of three notebooks at ₦2,000 each has a line amount of ₦6,000 before any separately established adjustment. This example does not specify a tax treatment.

Do not guess a tax rate, tax status or registration number. Where your transaction needs a tax treatment, use verified information appropriate to that transaction and seek clarification when it is uncertain. A polished invoice cannot resolve missing facts.

Before sending:

- Compare the customer name and reference with the order.
- Check quantities, prices, adjustments and currency.
- Verify payment instructions through your established business process.
- Remove internal notes and unrelated customer information.
- Open the exported file and read every page.

Save the exact version you sent. If an error is discovered later, preserve that version and create a clearly identified correction rather than silently changing the record. An invoice requests or records an amount due; it should not claim that payment was received unless that fact is separately established.

Suggested related template: Invoice. Next step: prepare the invoice, resolve missing facts, and review its saved preview before sending.

## GUIDE-C02 — Write a quotation that makes the offer clear

Status: DRAFT — EDP review and export checks pending.

A quotation helps a customer understand what you are offering and what the price covers. Its usefulness depends on the scope being clear enough for both sides to recognise the same work or goods.

Begin with the customer's request. Record the item or service, quantity, quality or specification, delivery location and any information the customer still needs to provide. Separate included work from optional extras. If the request is vague, ask a short follow-up question before presenting a firm amount.

List each priced item and show the currency. Include only charges, discounts and tax information that have been established for the proposed transaction. If your business chooses an offer-validity date, label it as your quoted date; do not present it as a legal deadline. State delivery or completion expectations accurately, including dependencies that could change them.

For a fictional printing request, “200 A5 booklets, 12 pages each, black-and-white interior, customer supplies final artwork” is more useful than “printing services.” If design and delivery are not included, say so. A customer can then compare the quotation with the actual request.

Review before sharing:

- Does every amount relate to a described item?
- Are quantities and units consistent?
- Are optional items clearly separated from the main total?
- Are assumptions and customer responsibilities visible?
- Is there a practical way to ask questions or accept the offer?

Keep a copy of the quotation and any later changes. When the customer requests something different, issue an updated version with a new revision reference and explain what changed. Preserve evidence of the customer's response without inventing a signature or acceptance.

A quotation is not proof of payment or proof that the work is finished. After an order is agreed, use the agreed details to prepare the next document and check for changes before carrying figures forward.

Suggested related templates: Quotation, Purchase Order, Invoice.

## GUIDE-C03 — Follow up an unpaid invoice with a useful reminder

Status: DRAFT — EDP review and export checks pending.

A helpful payment reminder tells the customer which invoice you mean, what your records currently show and how to resolve the matter. Check the records before drafting the message; a payment may have arrived without the reference you expected.

Compare the invoice with your payment records and any customer correspondence. Confirm the invoice number, original amount, payments already recorded and the remaining balance. Check whether the customer has raised a dispute or agreed a different payment arrangement. Do not call an amount overdue unless the relevant payment date is established and has passed.

Keep the first message short and specific. A fictional example is: “Our records currently show ₦18,000 outstanding on invoice INV-024. Please let us know if you have already paid, or if there is a question we can resolve.” Replace every detail with checked information. Do not add threats, interest, penalties or legal consequences that have not been properly established.

Before sending:

- Confirm the recipient is the appropriate contact.
- Check the balance and currency against your records.
- Refer to the correct saved invoice version.
- Include verified payment instructions only where needed.
- Ask for clarification without demanding unnecessary personal information.

Record when the reminder was sent and any response. If the customer supplies payment information, reconcile it through your normal process before marking the invoice paid. A screenshot or an assertion in a message should not automatically become a confirmed receipt in your records.

If the amount is disputed, record the disputed issue separately from the payment follow-up. Agree the next practical step with the customer rather than repeatedly sending an unchanged reminder. Any escalation should reflect your actual arrangements and appropriate advice for the situation.

Suggested related templates: Payment Reminder, Invoice, Receipt. Next step: verify the outstanding balance, review the draft and decide whether it is ready to send.

## GUIDE-C04 — Keep an inventory register people can maintain

Status: DRAFT — EDP review and export checks pending.

An inventory register is a record of what your business holds and how it moves. Start with a small set of fields that staff can update reliably, rather than a complicated form nobody completes.

Give each item a clear name and a consistent internal reference. Record the unit you count: pieces, cartons, metres or another suitable unit. Keep different units distinct. If one carton contains several pieces, document the conversion your business actually uses instead of mixing carton and piece quantities in the same column.

A useful movement record includes the date, item, quantity received or issued, location, supporting reference and person recording it. Separate damaged, returned or unavailable stock where those distinctions matter to your operation. Do not infer that a physical count proves ownership, product quality or regulatory approval.

For a fictional fabric shop, a register might show “blue cotton, roll B17, metres.” A sale of five metres is then recorded in the same unit as the stock balance. The business can trace the movement to its own sale reference without putting customer personal information into every stock row.

When counting stock:

- Decide the location and items to count.
- Record the time of the count and who performed it.
- Keep movements during the count identifiable.
- Compare the physical result with the recorded balance.
- Investigate differences before recording an explained adjustment.

Preserve the original entry and the reason for a correction. A difference may result from a unit mismatch, a delayed entry, damage or another cause; avoid assuming theft without evidence.

Choose a review routine suited to your business and assign responsibility for updates. The Word/PDF register is a recordkeeping aid, not an automated stock system: totals and movements need checking. Where volume makes manual work unreliable, review whether a more suitable operational tool is needed.

Suggested related templates: Inventory Register, Delivery Note, Purchase Order.

## GUIDE-C05 — Prepare an expense claim with a clear record

Status: DRAFT — EDP review and export checks pending.

An expense claim explains money someone says they spent for the business. It should help the person reviewing it understand the purpose, amount and supporting information without having to reconstruct the trip or purchase from scattered messages.

Record the claimant's name, the date of each expense, the supplier or service, the business purpose, amount and currency. Use one line for each distinct expense. If several costs belong to one activity, connect them with a clear reference while keeping the individual amounts visible.

Reference evidence your business is authorised to use, such as an available receipt or transaction record, and keep that evidence in its controlled location. Avoid including unrelated bank activity, private account details or other people's information. If evidence is missing, say so and explain what is known. Never create a substitute receipt or claim that a payment was verified when it was not.

For a fictional delivery visit, separate transport, parking and packing-material costs rather than writing “trip expenses” as one unexplained amount. If the business paid part of the cost directly, identify that portion so it is not reimbursed twice.

Before submitting:

- Check dates, amounts and currency.
- Explain the business purpose of each line.
- Identify any advance or amount already reimbursed.
- Match supporting references to the correct entries.
- Mark missing evidence and unresolved questions clearly.

The reviewer can record accepted, queried or declined items and the reason for the decision according to the business's actual policy. Approval of a claim and payment of a reimbursement are separate events. Record each accurately and preserve the reviewed version.

This guide does not determine whether an expense is deductible for tax, recoverable from another party or payable under an employment arrangement. Those questions need the relevant facts and applicable rules. The practical goal here is a record that can be checked and understood.

Suggested related template: Expense Claim Form.

## GUIDE-C06 — Connect purchase orders, deliveries and invoices

Status: DRAFT — EDP review and export checks pending.

Purchasing records are easier to follow when the request, delivery and bill refer to the same transaction. A purchase order describes what the business intends to buy. A delivery record describes what was actually received. An invoice records the supplier's request for payment. Keep these roles distinct.

Before placing an order, confirm the supplier, item specifications, quantities, prices, currency and agreed delivery arrangements. Give the order a reference that can also appear in your receiving record. Include only commitments your business has actually authorised.

When goods arrive, compare them with the order. Record the date, quantities received, visible condition and any shortages or differences. Avoid recording a complete delivery if only part arrived. If a person acknowledges receipt, describe what they are acknowledging rather than treating that acknowledgement as approval of every aspect of the transaction.

For a fictional order of ten storage boxes, eight delivered boxes should be recorded as eight received and two outstanding. If one delivered box is damaged, record that separately. The supplier's invoice may still need clarification before the business processes it.

Use this comparison checklist:

- Does the supplier and order reference match?
- Do descriptions and units refer to the same goods?
- What was ordered, received and invoiced?
- Are changes supported by an actual agreement?
- Which differences still need an answer?

Keep supporting correspondence alongside the transaction references. Record how a discrepancy was resolved, including any replacement, return or updated invoice. Do not silently change the original order to make a mismatch disappear.

Completion of this comparison does not itself prove payment or resolve all contractual questions. It provides an organised record for the business to decide its next step. Any payment approval should follow the business's own established process and verified payment instructions.

Suggested related templates: Purchase Order, Delivery Note, Invoice, Inventory Register.

## GUIDE-C07 — Turn meeting notes into clear actions

Status: DRAFT — EDP review and export checks pending.

Useful meeting notes help people remember what was discussed, what was decided and who will do the next piece of work. They do not need to reproduce every sentence spoken.

Record the meeting date, purpose and attendees accurately. Distinguish people who attended from those invited but absent. For each topic, capture the relevant decision, unresolved question and next action. Use neutral wording where views differed; do not present a proposal as a unanimous decision if that was not what happened.

Give each action one clear owner and, where agreed, a target date. A target date is an internal commitment, not automatically a statutory deadline. If nobody accepted responsibility, mark the owner as unresolved and seek clarification instead of assigning someone without agreement.

For a fictional stock-planning meeting, “Amina to compare two supplier quotations and report on Friday” is easier to act on than “look into supplies.” The supporting quotations can be linked by reference without copying unnecessary personal or commercial information into the notes.

Before circulating:

- Separate decisions from ideas still being considered.
- Check names, amounts and dates against the discussion.
- Identify each action owner and expected output.
- Remove irrelevant or sensitive personal commentary.
- Explain how attendees can request a correction.

Keep a dated version of the notes that was circulated. If a material correction is agreed, issue a revised version with a brief change note. Preserve the earlier version where your chosen recordkeeping process requires it, with access limited appropriately.

At the next meeting, review open actions and record their actual status. “Completed” should describe an observed outcome, not simply that the target date has passed. Ordinary meeting notes do not automatically satisfy formal board, company or nonprofit governance requirements; use the relevant properly reviewed document where such a requirement applies.

Suggested related template: Meeting Minutes. Next step: create an action list that each named owner can understand.

## GUIDE-C08 — Record a customer complaint and its resolution

Status: DRAFT — EDP review and export checks pending.

A complaint record helps your business respond consistently and understand recurring problems. Its first job is to capture what the customer reports without prematurely deciding whether the report is correct.

Record the date received, relevant order or service reference, contact method and a concise description of the issue. Ask what outcome the customer is seeking. Collect only details that help investigate or respond; avoid requesting identity documents or sensitive information simply because a form has space for them.

Acknowledge receipt through a channel your business actually monitors. Give a response expectation only if your business can support it. If you need more information, ask a specific question and explain why it matters. Do not describe an investigation as complete before the necessary checks have happened.

For a fictional complaint about an incomplete delivery, record what was ordered, what the customer says arrived and what your dispatch record shows. Keep those accounts separate until the difference is resolved. A neutral record is more useful than a label such as “difficult customer.”

Work through this checklist:

- Identify who owns the response.
- Gather the relevant transaction and delivery records.
- Record the customer's requested outcome.
- Note what has been checked and what remains uncertain.
- Record the agreed action and when it was actually completed.

If the matter involves safety, a suspected data incident or another specialist issue, route it through the appropriate internal process promptly. This guide does not prescribe a legal response time or determine refund rights.

When closing the record, explain the outcome and retain relevant evidence under your established retention approach. Do not mark the customer satisfied unless they actually said so. Review patterns periodically: repeated packaging damage or unclear order descriptions may point to a practical improvement the business can make.

Suggested related templates: Complaint Form, Incident Report, Delivery Note.

## GUIDE-C09 — Write a simple operating procedure people can follow

Status: DRAFT — EDP review and export checks pending.

A standard operating procedure describes a repeatable task clearly enough for someone with the necessary training to carry it out. Begin with one bounded activity, such as recording a delivery or preparing an order for dispatch.

State the purpose, when the procedure applies and who is responsible. List the information, equipment or permissions needed before starting. Then describe the steps in the order they happen, using short action verbs. Put a check at points where a mistake could change the outcome.

Describe what to do when the ordinary process cannot continue. A useful procedure includes a stop-and-escalate instruction for missing approval, damaged goods or inconsistent information. Do not invent technical safety instructions for hazardous work; those need appropriate competent input and applicable evidence.

For a fictional order-packing process, steps might be: locate the confirmed order, pick the listed items, compare quantities, check the package condition, record the dispatch reference and hand over through the agreed channel. The procedure should identify which actual business record proves each step was completed.

Test the draft:

- Can a suitably trained colleague find the starting point?
- Are responsibilities and required inputs clear?
- Are decisions explained rather than hidden in vague wording?
- Is there a route for exceptions and unanswered questions?
- Does the procedure match what staff can actually do?

Record the procedure's owner, version and review date. Ask the colleague who tested it where they hesitated, then improve those instructions. Keep the approved working copy easy to find and distinguish it from older versions.

A written procedure does not prove that staff followed it, that equipment is safe or that a regulated activity is authorised. Keep execution records and training arrangements appropriate to the task. When the process changes, update the procedure and explain the change to the people who use it.

Suggested related templates: Standard Operating Procedure, Staff Onboarding Checklist.

## GUIDE-C10 — Manage document versions without losing the history

Status: DRAFT — EDP review and export checks pending.

Version control helps a business answer a simple question: which document was used for this decision or transaction? It is especially useful when several people edit a quotation, proposal or policy.

Use a stable document reference and a clear version label. Record who prepared the revision, when it changed and what changed materially. Keep draft, approved, sent and replaced statuses distinct according to your actual workflow. A file called “final” is not evidence that anyone approved it.

Before editing a shared document, check that you are starting from the latest relevant version. If two people changed it, compare their work and resolve the differences deliberately. Do not overwrite another person's text simply because your copy was saved later.

For a fictional quotation, revision 2 might change the delivery location and add a separately priced service. Keep revision 1 identifiable and state which version the customer received. If the customer accepted an earlier version, do not silently replace that accepted wording with the new draft.

Use this practical routine:

- Give the document a recognisable reference.
- Save important revisions with a short change note.
- Record review or acceptance only when it happened.
- Export from the intended saved revision.
- Check the exported file before sending it.

Where a system restores an older version, preserve the newer history and label the restoration as a new event. Restoration of one document is different from restoring a whole business system after data loss; neither should be claimed unless demonstrated.

Limit editing access to people who need it. If a document is withdrawn or replaced, guide staff to the current version while retaining historical records appropriately. Avoid retaining unnecessary personal information indefinitely; follow your established retention and deletion decisions for the record involved.

Suggested related documents: Any quotation, proposal, policy or procedure with revisions. Next step: identify the current working copy and the last version actually shared.

## GUIDE-C11 — Hand over work without sharing passwords or losing context

Status: DRAFT — EDP review and export checks pending.

A useful handover lets the next person continue the work without guessing. Prepare it around open tasks and responsibilities, rather than a long description of everything the departing person has ever done.

List active work, current status, the next action, owner and any agreed target date. Point to the relevant business records and explain unresolved issues. Include dependencies, such as waiting for a customer answer or supplier delivery, so the receiving person understands why a task is paused.

Use approved access arrangements for the systems involved. Do not place passwords, recovery codes, private keys or unnecessary customer data in the handover document. Access should be granted through the business's authorised account-management process and removed when it is no longer needed.

For a fictional shop handover, one item might read: “Order ORD-031: customer confirmed quantity; supplier delivery date still unconfirmed; contact supplier using the saved business contact; record the response in the order file.” This gives the next person a specific action without claiming the goods have arrived.

Before the handover meeting:

- Separate completed tasks from open work.
- Check that linked records are the correct versions.
- Identify urgent decisions and explain their basis.
- Name the person accepting each responsibility.
- List access requests without including credentials.

Walk through a small sample of tasks together. Ask the receiving person to find the supporting record and explain the next step. This often reveals missing context before it causes a customer delay.

Record when the handover took place and any questions still unresolved. Assign an owner to those questions and update the working task list. A signed or acknowledged handover records the actual acknowledgement; it should not automatically claim that every task is complete, every asset is present or every employment issue has been resolved.

Suggested related templates: Staff Onboarding Checklist, Meeting Minutes, Inventory Register.

## GUIDE-C12 — Organise business records so you can find the answer

Status: DRAFT — EDP review and export checks pending.

Good organisation makes records easier to retrieve, review and protect. Start with the questions your business regularly needs to answer: what was agreed, what was delivered, what was paid and what remains to be done?

Choose a simple structure, such as customer transactions, supplier transactions, staff records, operating procedures and business administration. Give each transaction a consistent reference and use it across related documents. Keep personal or restricted records separate from material that a wider team needs to use.

Use descriptive filenames or document titles that include the relevant reference, date and version where helpful. Avoid relying on a person's memory or a single chat thread as the only way to locate a record. Make sure staff know which system holds the current working copy.

For a fictional purchase, an order, delivery note, invoice and payment record can share the internal reference PUR-018. Each remains a separate document with its own meaning. Linking them does not turn an invoice into proof of payment or an incomplete delivery into a completed order.

Check the arrangement:

- Can an authorised colleague find one complete transaction?
- Are drafts distinguishable from versions actually sent?
- Are access permissions appropriate to the information?
- Are corrections and unresolved differences visible?
- Is there an agreed process for backup and recovery?

Test retrieval with a recent example instead of assuming the folder structure works. Also test recovery through an authorised process; copying a file once does not prove that all records can be restored when needed.

Set retention and deletion decisions by record type using the business's actual needs and applicable verified requirements. This guide does not specify a universal number of years. Avoid keeping unnecessary personal data simply because storage is available. When records are removed, preserve any appropriate deletion record without retaining the information that was meant to be deleted.

Suggested related templates: Compliance Evidence Checklist, Inventory Register, Meeting Minutes.
