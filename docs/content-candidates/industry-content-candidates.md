## Industry landing pages

### retail/wholesale

Start with the journey from a supplier order to money received. A clear record of what you bought, what arrived, what you sold and what remains in stock makes everyday decisions easier. Separate a customer's quotation from their invoice, and record a receipt only when the payment details have been confirmed. For wholesale orders, capture quantities, delivery arrangements and the person who can resolve a discrepancy before goods leave your premises.

Use this page to organise purchasing, stock movements, deliveries and customer follow-up. Ask: do you sell directly to consumers, supply other businesses, or do both? Do customers buy on credit? Who checks delivered quantities? Do you import, repackage or sell products under your own brand? Those answers help identify the next documents and research questions without treating every retailer as the same business.

**Template connections:** TPL-04 Purchase order; TPL-07 Inventory register; TPL-05 Delivery note; TPL-01 Invoice; TPL-03 Receipt; TPL-12 Payment reminder letter; TPL-09 Customer complaint form.

**Candidate source leads:** CAC; NRS; product-specific NAFDAC research only where the actual activity warrants it.

### packaged-food manufacturing

Build a record around each product and production batch. Capture the ingredients you use, their suppliers, the version of your production instructions, the packaging selected and where the finished goods go. Keeping these connections visible can help your team investigate a complaint and locate related stock. Record what actually happened; a completed form is not proof that a product is safe or approved.

Begin with practical questions: what food do you make, and how is it processed and packaged? Is production in your own facility or a shared or contracted facility? Which steps depend on temperature control? Can you trace a delivered batch to its ingredients and customers? Use the answers to prepare an operational document set and identify the specific official material that needs checking before any regulatory guidance is shown.

**Template connections:** TPL-35 Standard operating procedure; TPL-07 Inventory register; TPL-37 Food hygiene checklist; TPL-36 Product recall plan; TPL-38 Equipment maintenance log; TPL-09 Customer complaint form; TPL-40 Compliance evidence checklist.

**Candidate source leads:** NAFDAC and NAFDAC food inspection; CAC. Product classifications, inspection routes and source currentness remain review questions.

### restaurants/hospitality

Organise the work that repeats every day: receiving supplies, preparing service, assigning responsibilities, recording incidents and responding to guests. A simple handover can tell the next shift what is running low, what needs attention and which customer issue remains open. Keep the record proportionate to the operation, whether you run a small restaurant, a catering service or accommodation with several service teams.

Ask which activities you actually perform. Do you prepare meals for immediate service, supply catered events, manufacture packaged products, or combine these activities? Do you manage guest rooms or provide transport? Who checks supplier deliveries and follows up equipment problems? These distinctions shape the documents you need. They also prevent a manufacturing checklist or another sector's guidance from being presented as the complete answer for your premises.

**Template connections:** TPL-04 Purchase order; TPL-07 Inventory register; TPL-35 Standard operating procedure; TPL-37 Food hygiene checklist; TPL-38 Equipment maintenance log; TPL-09 Customer complaint form; TPL-34 Incident report.

**Candidate source leads:** CAC; NAFDAC manufacturing sources only if a distinct packaged-product activity is identified. Local-premises guidance needs separate evidence.

### agriculture

Give each farming activity a clear operating record. You may want to track inputs purchased, stock on hand, equipment use, supplier deliveries and the terms of each sale. Where you operate several sites or handle several products, use separate references so that costs and movements can be understood later. Record dates and quantities as they occur instead of trying to reconstruct them at the end of a season.

Ask: are you growing crops, raising animals, processing produce, storing goods or transporting them for others? Are sales local, wholesale or intended for export? Who supplies your inputs, and who confirms quantities when produce changes hands? This page connects everyday records to those activities. It does not offer agronomic prescriptions, certify inputs or assume that farming and food processing follow the same regulatory route.

**Template connections:** TPL-04 Purchase order; TPL-07 Inventory register; TPL-05 Delivery note; TPL-38 Equipment maintenance log; TPL-06 Expense claim form; TPL-01 Invoice; TPL-34 Incident report.

**Candidate source leads:** CAC; NAFDAC only for an identified processing/product activity. Agricultural and export-specific sources remain to be identified and reviewed.

### general manufacturing

Connect the customer order to purchasing, production, inspection records and delivery. A practical document set helps people know which specification they are working to, which materials were received and what changed during the job. Use versioned operating instructions and record exceptions rather than quietly replacing the original plan. Equipment maintenance records can also help explain interruptions and recurring problems.

Start by describing the actual product and process. Do you make a finished product, assemble components, repackage goods or manufacture for another brand? Which materials and outsourced steps need traceable records? Who accepts the finished work, and how are rejected items handled? These questions support a more useful workflow than a generic manufacturing label. Product-specific standards, approvals and technical safety requirements need their own authoritative evidence and assessment.

**Template connections:** TPL-04 Purchase order; TPL-24 Procurement evaluation form; TPL-35 Standard operating procedure; TPL-07 Inventory register; TPL-38 Equipment maintenance log; TPL-05 Delivery note; TPL-34 Incident report.

**Candidate source leads:** CAC; relevant product authorities to be identified from the product and activity. No universal standards certification claim is made.

### logistics/transport

Make the movement of goods easy to follow from collection to delivery. Record the customer instruction, item description, quantity, destination and the handover details your team actually confirms. When a delivery changes, keep the original instruction and the update together. A clear record of damage, delay or a missing item helps your team investigate without inventing a recipient's signature or proof of delivery.

Ask what service you provide: your own deliveries, transport for other businesses, warehousing, dispatch coordination or a combination. Who holds the goods at each stage? Do subcontractors complete any journeys? What information do drivers need, and who can see customer contact details? Use these answers to connect service documents, operational procedures and incident records. Vehicle, route, cargo and location-specific requirements need separate research before recommendations become available.

**Template connections:** TPL-13 Service agreement; TPL-23 Statement of work; TPL-05 Delivery note; TPL-35 Standard operating procedure; TPL-34 Incident report; TPL-38 Equipment maintenance log; TPL-01 Invoice.

**Candidate source leads:** CAC; NDPC discovery lead. Transport-specific authorities and operative rules remain to be researched for the actual service.

### construction

Keep the agreed scope, the work performed and the payment request connected. Before a job starts, describe deliverables, responsibilities, materials and the milestones the parties intend to use. During the job, record site issues and proposed changes with dates and named responsibilities. A revised plan should remain distinguishable from the original instruction, especially when a change affects cost or timing.

Ask whether you act as developer, main contractor, subcontractor, consultant or supplier. Which premises and locations are involved? Who can accept work and authorise changes? Are tools, equipment or materials provided by another party? This page helps organise proposals, scopes, purchasing and records of incidents. It does not provide engineering design, certify site safety, establish permission to build or turn a general contract template into a project-specific legal opinion.

**Template connections:** TPL-22 Business proposal; TPL-23 Statement of work; TPL-02 Quotation; TPL-04 Purchase order; TPL-21 Independent contractor agreement; TPL-34 Incident report; TPL-33 Health and safety checklist.

**Candidate source leads:** CAC; location-specific planning and professional sources require separate discovery. Contract and safety material requires enhanced review.

### real estate

Separate the activities within your property business. Marketing a property, managing it for an owner, arranging maintenance and occupying premises yourself create different document needs. Keep the instruction you received alongside the scope of your service, relevant correspondence and any agreed payment milestones. When handling a complaint or maintenance issue, record the report, the next action and the person responsible for following it up.

Ask: do you act as an agent, property manager, developer, owner or tenant? Which state and locality is the property in? Who is entitled to give instructions, and what evidence supports that role? Use this page to prepare service and premises questions before choosing documents. A checklist can organise an enquiry; it does not verify title, determine tenancy rights, validate a transaction or replace property-specific legal work.

**Template connections:** TPL-13 Service agreement; TPL-23 Statement of work; TPL-39 Commercial lease checklist; TPL-09 Customer complaint form; TPL-01 Invoice; TPL-03 Receipt; TPL-40 Compliance evidence checklist.

**Candidate source leads:** CAC; state/property-specific sources to be identified. Do not infer one tenancy or land rule for all Nigerian locations.

### technology/software

Make the boundaries of each project understandable before development begins. Describe the problem, the deliverables, what the customer supplies and how feedback will be handled. Keep proposed changes visible so that a new request is not silently treated as part of the original scope. When your service handles information about people, first map what enters the service, where it goes and who can access it.

Ask whether you sell a subscription product, build software for clients, provide technical support or process information for another organisation. Which outside services are involved? What happens when a customer leaves? Use these answers to connect project documents, operating procedures and privacy-related candidates. A generated notice or agreement does not establish your actual data practices, ownership arrangements or security posture; those details need evidence and the applicable review.

**Template connections:** TPL-23 Statement of work; TPL-13 Service agreement; TPL-15 Non-disclosure agreement; TPL-29 Privacy notice; TPL-31 Data processing agreement; TPL-32 Data retention policy; TPL-35 Standard operating procedure.

**Candidate source leads:** CAC; NDPC discovery lead. Privacy and contractual claims remain subject to enhanced evidence checks.

### e-commerce

Follow an order from the product listing to payment, fulfilment and any later complaint. Keep the description the customer saw connected to the item supplied, the delivery arrangement and the payment record. If something changes, make the change visible to the people handling the order. Distinguish an order acknowledgement from a receipt and a dispatch record from confirmed delivery.

Ask whether you own the goods, operate a marketplace, sell for other merchants or arrange delivery. Who handles returns and customer support? Do you import, repackage or put your brand on a product? Which customer details are shared with payment and delivery providers? These answers help organise the right document set. Product rules, consumer-facing terms and data practices need their own review; a storefront alone does not answer those questions.

**Template connections:** TPL-01 Invoice; TPL-03 Receipt; TPL-05 Delivery note; TPL-09 Customer complaint form; TPL-30 Website terms and conditions; TPL-29 Privacy notice; TPL-07 Inventory register.

**Candidate source leads:** CAC; NDPC discovery lead; product-specific NAFDAC sources where relevant. Consumer-protection instruments need additional source capture.

### beauty/personal care

Distinguish providing a service from making, importing or selling a product. A salon may need clear service records, stock records and a way to handle complaints, while a cosmetics producer needs a different set of production and product records. If you do both, keep the activities visible rather than treating one label as a complete description of the business.

Ask which treatments or services you provide and which products you manufacture, mix, repackage or sell under your own name. Who supplies those products? How do you record a customer concern and identify the item involved? Use this page to organise purchases, procedures, equipment care and follow-up. It does not assess a treatment's suitability, validate ingredient safety or establish that a marketing claim is supported. Those questions require appropriate evidence and a separate review.

**Template connections:** TPL-07 Inventory register; TPL-09 Customer complaint form; TPL-35 Standard operating procedure; TPL-38 Equipment maintenance log; TPL-34 Incident report; TPL-04 Purchase order; TPL-40 Compliance evidence checklist.

**Candidate source leads:** CAC; NAFDAC cosmetics for actual product-manufacturing activity. Service-only operations need separately scoped research.

### fashion/textiles

Keep the customer's specification close to the order record. Capture the item, material, measurements supplied, agreed changes and the date the work is expected to be ready. For a production run, connect material purchases and stock movements to the styles or batches being made. Record an acceptance or alteration request only when the customer has actually provided it.

Ask whether you offer bespoke tailoring, manufacture garments, sell finished items or produce for another brand. Who owns or supplies the designs and fabrics? Are outside makers involved? How are variations in colour, sizing or finishing handled? This page helps connect quotations, purchasing, delivery and customer follow-up. It does not determine intellectual-property ownership, certify fabric performance or promise that a standard sales document resolves every disagreement about bespoke work.

**Template connections:** TPL-02 Quotation; TPL-04 Purchase order; TPL-07 Inventory register; TPL-23 Statement of work; TPL-19 Sales agreement; TPL-05 Delivery note; TPL-09 Customer complaint form.

**Candidate source leads:** CAC; contractual and design-rights sources to be identified where claims are proposed.

### healthcare

Use this workspace for the business administration around your service. Purchasing, equipment maintenance, staff onboarding and handling a reported incident all benefit from clear responsibilities and reliable records. Keep administrative documents separate from clinical assessment and treatment decisions. A general business form should not be used to invent a patient history, record consent that was not obtained or imply that a practitioner or facility has been verified.

Ask what service you actually provide, which professionals deliver it and which locations are involved. Do you also manufacture, import or sell regulated products? What personal information would a document contain, and who should have access? These answers help identify operational templates and the specific professional, facility and product evidence that needs checking. Clinical content and sensitive patient records require separately approved systems and controls.

**Template connections:** TPL-04 Purchase order; TPL-38 Equipment maintenance log; TPL-11 Staff onboarding checklist; TPL-34 Incident report; TPL-35 Standard operating procedure; TPL-40 Compliance evidence checklist.

**Candidate source leads:** CAC; NDPC discovery lead. Relevant health-profession, facility and product authorities remain activity-specific research requirements.

### education

Organise the administration that supports teaching or training. Keep supplier purchases, staff onboarding, meeting decisions and customer enquiries in a form the responsible person can find and update. For a training engagement, describe the audience, delivery format, materials and agreed outputs before work begins. Record attendance, feedback or completion only when it reflects what actually happened.

Ask whether you run a school, training business, tutoring service or online learning product. Do you work with children or adults? Who pays for the service, and what information about learners is collected? Are classes delivered at your premises, a customer's location or online? These distinctions help select useful business documents. This page does not verify accreditation, grant permission to operate, provide safeguarding certification or establish how sensitive learner information should be handled.

**Template connections:** TPL-22 Business proposal; TPL-23 Statement of work; TPL-13 Service agreement; TPL-11 Staff onboarding checklist; TPL-08 Meeting minutes; TPL-09 Customer complaint form; TPL-29 Privacy notice.

**Candidate source leads:** CAC; NDPC discovery lead. Education and safeguarding authorities require source research matched to the actual service and location.

### professional services

Make the engagement clear before promising a result. A useful proposal explains the customer's problem, the work you will undertake, what is outside scope and which information the customer must supply. Turn the agreed scope into practical milestones and keep later requests distinguishable from the original engagement. Invoice against confirmed work or the payment arrangement the parties actually agreed.

Ask what service you offer, who performs it and whether the work depends on particular professional credentials. Are you advising, implementing, preparing records or arranging introductions? Will subcontractors or outside tools receive client information? These answers shape the documents and research needed. A general consultancy template is a starting point for review; it does not confer professional status, guarantee an outcome or authorise Valo to perform a regulated service on your behalf.

**Template connections:** TPL-22 Business proposal; TPL-14 Consultancy agreement; TPL-23 Statement of work; TPL-15 Non-disclosure agreement; TPL-01 Invoice; TPL-12 Payment reminder letter; TPL-08 Meeting minutes.

**Candidate source leads:** CAC; NDPC discovery lead; the actual profession's governing sources need separate identification and review.

### media/creative

Connect the creative brief to the work the client expects to receive. Record the audience, format, deliverables, review stages and the materials each party supplies. Keep requested revisions visible so that a new concept or additional output can be discussed clearly. When contributors, locations or third-party assets are involved, record their source and the permissions actually obtained rather than assuming that access means permission to reuse.

Ask whether you create content, organise productions, manage advertising or distribute other people's work. Who supplies music, images, footage and brand materials? Which platforms and uses are intended? Use these answers to prepare project, contractor and purchasing records. This page does not clear copyright, certify advertising claims or invent releases and permissions. Those questions need the relevant evidence and a properly scoped review.

### import/export

Build a consistent record for each shipment before goods move. Keep the product description, supplier or customer order, quantities, agreed delivery arrangements and supporting documents together. Record which party prepares each document and who checks discrepancies. Where a shipment contains several products or suppliers, use references that preserve those differences instead of treating the entire consignment as one undifferentiated item.

Ask what goods are involved, which countries they move between and what role your business performs. Are you the manufacturer, trader, agent or logistics provider? Do products need temperature control or special handling? This page connects purchasing, stock and delivery records to those questions. It does not assign tariff codes, calculate duties, certify origin, approve restricted goods or submit customs filings. Each of those decisions requires its own authoritative evidence and authorised process.

**Template connections:** TPL-04 Purchase order; TPL-18 Supplier agreement; TPL-19 Sales agreement; TPL-07 Inventory register; TPL-05 Delivery note; TPL-01 Invoice; TPL-40 Compliance evidence checklist.

Describe the service before choosing the paperwork. Selling equipment, installing a system, maintaining assets, operating a supply service and collecting customer payments involve different records and responsibilities. Keep the customer's instruction, the equipment involved and the work performed connected. A maintenance log should record actual activity and findings, while an incident report should preserve the facts known at the time.

Ask what you own or operate, where the service is delivered and who receives it. Do you use contractors, manage meters or sell fuel or other physical products? Who handles faults, customer complaints and payment disputes? These answers help connect project and operational documents. ### financial services

Start by describing the financial activity precisely. Lending, collecting repayments, providing software to a lender and offering bookkeeping support are different services, even when their websites use similar language. Keep operational instructions and customer communications connected to the activity you actually perform. Record complaints and incidents clearly, and distinguish a requested transaction from a completed one supported by reliable evidence.

Ask whether your business holds customer money, initiates payments, makes credit decisions, introduces customers or supplies technology. Who is responsible for each step, and which outside providers are involved? This page helps organise business records and identify evidence questions. It does not grant financial authorisation, recommend investments, approve credit or establish that a partner's permission covers your own activities. Sensitive customer information and financial operations need separately reviewed controls.

### other

Describe your business in your own words when none of the listed categories fits. Start with what you make or do, who buys it, where the work happens and how customers pay. If you combine several activities, keep each visible. A business can sell products, provide a service and operate from more than one location without fitting neatly into one category.

Ask which event you are trying to organise now: making an offer, buying supplies, delivering work, recording a payment, hiring someone or investigating a problem. Begin with documents connected to that event, then add more only when the need is clear. This page provides a practical starting point and records unanswered questions. It does not treat an unclassified activity as unregulated or imply that missing coverage means no obligations apply.

**Template connections:** TPL-22 Business proposal; TPL-02 Quotation; TPL-01 Invoice; TPL-04 Purchase order; TPL-09 Customer complaint form; TPL-08 Meeting minutes; TPL-40 Compliance evidence checklist.

## Six deeper-pack candidates

Each pack below is **DRAFT pending EDP**. Steps are optional operational organisation suggestions, not a complete compliance checklist. Connections to enhanced-risk templates remain conditional on their own eligible versions and suitability for the proposed use.

### retail/wholesale — deeper pack

**Purpose:** Build a consistent purchase-to-sale trail without asking a small team to maintain the same information in several disconnected places.

1. **Record the order.** Use TPL-04 Purchase order to capture the supplier, items, quantities and agreed delivery information. Keep confirmation and later changes with the order rather than silently replacing it.
2. **Check what arrived.** Compare the physical delivery with the order and supporting delivery record. Enter confirmed stock movements in TPL-07 Inventory register; record discrepancies for follow-up instead of adjusting them without explanation.
3. **Prepare the sale.** Use TPL-02 Quotation where a customer needs an offer and TPL-01 Invoice for the agreed transaction. Keep discounts, delivery charges and any tax information dependent on confirmed inputs.
4. **Complete the handover and payment record.** Connect TPL-05 Delivery note to the order. Use TPL-03 Receipt only for confirmed payment details; keep an unpaid balance distinguishable from money received.
5. **Resolve the exception.** Use TPL-09 Customer complaint form for an issue and TPL-12 Payment reminder letter for a factual payment follow-up. Record the next action and owner without inventing penalties or admissions.

**Questions to resolve:** Retail, wholesale or both? Cash or credit sales? Own-brand products, imports or repackaging? Multiple stock locations? Who can approve a discount or resolve a discrepancy?

**Candidate source leads:** CAC and NRS; product-specific authorities only after the product and activity are known. Fees, tax treatment and legal duties remain outside this operational draft.

### packaged-food manufacturing — deeper pack

**Purpose:** Connect product instructions, materials, production records and distribution so that the team can explain what happened to a particular batch.

1. **Describe the product and operation.** Record ingredients, processing, packaging, intended storage and the actual manufacturing arrangement. Identify whether the work happens at your premises, a shared facility or a contracted producer before choosing a regulatory research route.
2. **Prepare the working instructions.** Use TPL-35 Standard operating procedure for the team's actual process. TPL-37 Food hygiene checklist can organise checks once reviewed for the operation; do not invent temperatures, shelf lives or technical safety limits.
3. **Record materials and equipment activity.** Link TPL-04 Purchase order and TPL-07 Inventory register to supplier references and batch movements. Use TPL-38 Equipment maintenance log to preserve actual servicing and fault records.
4. **Connect each dispatch.** Use TPL-05 Delivery note with product and batch references where appropriate. Keep distribution records usable for tracing goods without claiming that a completed record proves safety.
5. **Handle complaints and possible withdrawal.** Record facts with TPL-09 Customer complaint form and TPL-34 Incident report. A reviewed TPL-36 Product recall plan can organise responsibilities; actual technical and authority decisions need the relevant evidence and process.

**Questions to resolve:** Which product and process? Any temperature-sensitive stages? Who owns the product registration evidence? Can a batch be traced to materials and recipients? What source gaps remain?

**Candidate source leads:** NAFDAC, NAFDAC food inspection and CAC. Simplified-scheme exclusions are not general exemptions. Do not use historical tariffs or draft regulations as operative rules.

### restaurants/hospitality — deeper pack

**Purpose:** Give a busy team a repeatable way to prepare for service, hand over work and follow up issues while keeping different business activities distinct.

1. **Map the services.** List restaurant service, catering, accommodation and any packaged-product production separately. Note premises and shifts, the people responsible and which activities are delivered by outside suppliers.
2. **Prepare purchasing and stock records.** Connect TPL-04 Purchase order to expected deliveries and TPL-07 Inventory register to confirmed stock movements. Record shortages and substitutions so that the next shift can understand them.
3. **Set out the daily routine.** Use TPL-35 Standard operating procedure for opening, service handover and closing. A reviewed TPL-37 Food hygiene checklist can support the relevant food activities without pretending to be a complete safety assessment.
4. **Track equipment and service issues.** Record actual faults and maintenance in TPL-38 Equipment maintenance log. Use TPL-34 Incident report for an event needing follow-up, preserving known facts and separating them from assumptions.
5. **Close the customer loop.** Record a guest concern in TPL-09 Customer complaint form. For catered work, connect TPL-02 Quotation, TPL-01 Invoice and a confirmed TPL-03 Receipt to the same engagement.

**Questions to resolve:** Immediate meal service or packaged manufacture? One premises or several? Events with custom scope? Who hands over unresolved issues? What personal information is retained about guests?

**Candidate source leads:** CAC; NAFDAC manufacturing material only for the relevant product activity. Local premises, service-specific and privacy guidance needs its own authoritative evidence.

### technology/software — deeper pack

**Purpose:** Keep product commitments, delivery decisions and information-handling practices aligned as a project or service changes.

1. **Describe the engagement.** Use TPL-22 Business proposal to explain the intended outcome and TPL-23 Statement of work to record deliverables, assumptions, responsibilities and exclusions supplied by the parties.
2. **Prepare the relevant agreement candidates.** Connect the scope to TPL-13 Service agreement or TPL-14 Consultancy agreement as appropriate. Use TPL-15 Non-disclosure agreement only within its reviewed assumptions; do not assume it resolves ownership or every confidentiality issue.
3. **Map information and outside services.** Record what data enters the service, who uses it, where it is sent and what happens when a customer leaves. These facts inform TPL-29 Privacy notice, TPL-31 Data processing agreement and TPL-32 Data retention policy candidates.
4. **Record delivery and changes.** Use TPL-08 Meeting minutes for actual decisions and TPL-35 Standard operating procedure for recurring support or release work. Keep new requests distinguishable from the agreed scope.
5. **Follow through after handover.** Connect TPL-01 Invoice to the agreed payment basis. Use TPL-09 Customer complaint form or TPL-34 Incident report to record issues and ownership of follow-up without fabricating security or availability claims.

**Questions to resolve:** Client project or subscription service? Who supplies content? Which providers receive data? Who accepts delivery? What is the actual exit and deletion process?

**Candidate source leads:** CAC and NDPC discovery lead. Contract, privacy and transfer conclusions require enhanced review; this pack supplies questions, not answers to those legal decisions.

### professional services — deeper pack

**Purpose:** Make an engagement understandable to the client and the team, from the first proposal to the final follow-up.

1. **Describe the request.** Use TPL-22 Business proposal to capture the client's stated need, intended deliverables and information still missing. Separate confirmed facts from assumptions and avoid promising a result outside your control.
2. **Define the work.** Use TPL-23 Statement of work for tasks, milestones, responsibilities and exclusions. Record who can give instructions and how additional work will be discussed.
3. **Prepare the engagement documents.** Link TPL-14 Consultancy agreement and, where relevant, TPL-15 Non-disclosure agreement to the actual scope. Check that the candidate's assumptions fit the service and that no professional credential or permission is invented.
4. **Maintain the working record.** Use TPL-08 Meeting minutes for decisions and TPL-06 Expense claim form for actual reimbursable spending under the agreed arrangement. Preserve changes and client responses with the engagement.
5. **Close and follow up.** Use TPL-01 Invoice and TPL-12 Payment reminder letter with confirmed amounts and dates. Use TPL-09 Customer complaint form to record concerns and agreed next steps rather than silently rewriting earlier records.

**Questions to resolve:** Advice, implementation or administration? Any regulated activity? Who performs the work? Are subcontractors involved? Which client information is shared with outside tools?

**Candidate source leads:** CAC, NDPC discovery lead and the relevant profession's authoritative sources once identified. No service or individual is described as licensed or verified by this pack.

### beauty/personal care — deeper pack

**Purpose:** Separate service delivery from product activity, then build a practical record of supplies, procedures and customer concerns.

1. **List the actual activities.** Distinguish salon or personal-care services from manufacturing, mixing, repackaging, importing and resale. Identify products sold under your name and products supplied by others.
2. **Connect purchasing and stock.** Use TPL-04 Purchase order and TPL-07 Inventory register to record suppliers, items received and movements. Retain product references that may help identify an item involved in a later complaint.
3. **Describe the working routine.** Use TPL-35 Standard operating procedure for the actual operational steps and TPL-38 Equipment maintenance log for cleaning, servicing or faults that the team records. Do not generate treatment protocols or unsupported safety assurances.
4. **Record and follow up concerns.** Use TPL-09 Customer complaint form and TPL-34 Incident report for the customer's account, the facts known and the next action. Avoid invented diagnoses, consent or conclusions about causation.
5. **Prepare evidence questions for product activity.** Use TPL-40 Compliance evidence checklist to organise actual supplier and product records. Manufacturing-related guidance, labelling or a TPL-36 Product recall plan needs separately reviewed scope; a service-only salon is not automatically a manufacturer.

**Questions to resolve:** Services, products or both? Own-brand items? Imported or locally sourced products? Who handles complaints? What sensitive information is collected and who can access it?
