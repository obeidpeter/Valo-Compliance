## GUD-19 — Find the food-product route that matches your activity

### Customer body starts

Start with what your business actually does. Making a packaged sauce, importing a finished drink, storing someone else's ingredients and serving a prepared meal involve different facts. Write down the product, whether it is packaged, where it is made, your role, the site and the intended market. An industry label alone cannot choose the right application.

NAFDAC publishes separate guidance for micro-scale, small-scale and other pre-packaged food manufacturing facilities. Its current micro-scale guide contains inconsistent staff descriptions in different sections. Do not settle your route by selecting whichever number looks convenient. A product excluded from a simplified scheme is not thereby exempt from regulation.

Prepare a short enquiry with the actual product description, process, staffing, equipment and site arrangements. Ask the relevant official service to confirm the applicable route and retain the response with the guide version used. If you use another business to manufacture or pack, identify both businesses and who owns the product and brand; do not hide the arrangement behind a supplier invoice.

Keep a small decision record: question raised, facts supplied, official response, date and next action. Recheck it when the product, premises, production arrangement or market changes. A route enquiry is not an application approval, and an application receipt is not a product certificate.

This guidance helps organise that enquiry. It does not determine every national, state or local operating requirement, and it does not establish that every restaurant, salon or retailer needs the same product-manufacturing approval.

### Customer body ends

**Internal claim map:** PROD-S01 §1.1–1.7, physical PDF p.2 and Personnel §1.1 p.4; PROD-S02 §1 p.2; PROD-S03 §1.1 p.2. Enquiry/decision-record method is original practical guidance. Required applicability inputs: product, activity, packaging, origin, site, staffing, manufacturing arrangement. ## GUD-20 — Prepare a food facility inspection file

### Customer body starts

For a business manufacturing or packing pre-packaged food in Nigeria, prepare the inspection file around the actual premises and products. NAFDAC's facility guide links the application to documents, an inspection and subsequent review. A well-organised file helps answer questions; it does not guarantee a satisfactory inspection.

Create an index showing each requested item, its owner, the version supplied and any unresolved gap. Match the business identity, site address, product description, packaging artwork and supporting technical records across the file. Explain a mismatch rather than editing an old certificate to make it appear current. Where another business performs manufacturing, identify the real arrangement and the relevant agreement.

Walk through the process with the people responsible for receiving materials, making the product, checking it, cleaning and dispatch. Make sure a written procedure describes the work actually performed. Keep training and maintenance records linked to the correct people, equipment and site. These organising steps support preparation; they do not replace the guide's full applicable document list or a technical assessment.

Record inspection correspondence separately from your internal checklist. A proposed visit date can change. If the Agency raises a concern, track its exact wording, response, supporting evidence and current status. Do not mark it resolved solely because your team uploaded a reply.

Use the current official application route and confirm charges through official instructions. This guide supplies no fee, approval promise or medical-test prescription. Store any legitimately required staff-health evidence through a restricted process, with only the necessary reference in the general inspection index.

### Customer body ends

**Internal claim map:** PROD-S03 §2 physical PDF pp.2–4 (facility/application evidence), §§4–5 p.5 (scheduling, inspection and review). Exact current clinical requirements are outside this entry; no source-derived test list copied. Preparation index/walkthrough/correspondence tracking are original recommendations. ## GUD-21 — Track food registration without mistaking progress for approval

### Customer body starts

If you are applying to register a pre-packaged food product made in Nigeria, keep the product application separate from the business's general registration and internal readiness work. NAFDAC's March 2026 guide distinguishes application review, approval consideration and issue of a product certificate. Filing a form or paying a fee does not confer registration status.

Create one record for the specific product and application reference. Link the exact product name, manufacturer, formulation or specification where applicable, label version and documents submitted. Keep the latest official correspondence beside the version it concerns. If a document changes, record the change and check how it should be handled before assuming an earlier submission covers it.

Useful internal statuses include preparing, submitted, query received, response submitted and official decision received. Choose them from actual events. Record a certificate only after receiving it, and check its product identity, conditions and validity details. A screenshot showing an upload or payment should remain evidence of that step only.

For each outstanding question, assign an owner and retain the answer sent. Do not promise a launch date based on an unverified processing estimate: the current guide's processing-time sentence contains inconsistent words and numerals. Confirm the current position through the official route.

This entry addresses the local pre-packaged product route. Imported products and different product classes need their own applicable guidance. Product registration also does not automatically provide advertisement permission. Before launch, keep any separate permissions and conditions visible instead of treating one certificate as approval for every activity.

### Customer body ends

**Internal claim map:** PROD-S04 §§1–2 physical PDF p.2, §§5–6 and 8.2/8.4/8.6 p.3. Source-backed claims: local product scope, distinct decision/certificate stages, filing/payment not registration, advertisement separate and unresolved timeline text. ## GUD-22 — Make a food batch traceable before a problem occurs

### Customer body starts

A useful traceability record lets the business connect a particular product batch with its inputs and destinations. NAFDAC's food manufacturing guidance calls for distribution information that identifies the product, batch, recipient, dispatch date and quantity. A general sales total cannot answer which recipients received an affected lot.

Choose a consistent batch or lot identifier and carry it through the records that already describe the work. Check a real example from incoming materials through production, storage and dispatch. If two systems use different references, record the link explicitly. Do not assume that matching dates prove that records belong to the same batch.

For a practical check, select one completed batch and ask a colleague to locate its supporting records without relying on your memory. Can they identify what was sent, how much, to whom and where the remaining stock is held? Record gaps and assign someone to resolve them. Keep any practice exercise clearly labelled so it does not become a false recall or customer notification.

Use one stated unit when reconciling quantities. Packs, cartons and weight are not interchangeable without a documented conversion. Returned stock has moved from one location to another; it is not an additional quantity to add to the original dispatch total. Distinguish a recipient contact from a physical stock count.

Limit access to customer contact details while keeping them available to authorised response staff. This is an organising method for the identified food operation, not a guarantee that a batch is safe or that every required record has been captured.

### Customer body ends

**Internal claim map:** PROD-S05 §32.1(a–b), physical PDF p.53; §39, physical PDF p.59. Narrow source fact: distribution/lot traceability information. Exercise, unit reconciliation and system-link method are original recommendations. No arbitrary retention period or universal product-sector traceability law is asserted. Linkable candidate tools: TPL-07 and food-scoped TPL-36 only after their own eligibility.

## GUD-23 — Organise the first response to a food-product concern

### Customer body starts

Treat a reported problem as information to assess, not as proof that every unit is affected or that the product is harmless. Record what was reported, when it arrived, the product and lot details, the available evidence and who is coordinating the response. Keep the reporter's account distinct from findings established later.

NAFDAC's food manufacturing guidance expects a defined recall procedure with responsible people, tracing and effectiveness checks. It also calls for authority involvement in serious or potentially life-threatening situations. An urgent concern should reach the designated response lead promptly; completing a form must not delay the appropriate emergency or authority response.

The authorised team should record the scope of any investigation, hold, distribution restriction or recall decision and its basis. Include related products or batches in the assessment where the evidence makes them relevant. Do not let a generic template choose a recall class, reporting deadline, public statement or product-release decision.

Prepare communications from the verified facts and actual instructions. Identify the product clearly, state the action recipients should take and provide a genuine contact route. Retain the approved message and evidence of delivery. Drafting a message does not prove that anyone received it.

Keep physical quantities, recipient responses and unresolved tracing gaps separate. Record follow-up until the responsible decision-maker has evidence for the next step. This entry covers food operations; medical products and other sectors require their own procedures. It does not authorise destruction, donation, reprocessing or resale of affected stock, nor does a business closure note replace any required authority decision.

### Customer body ends

**Internal claim map:** PROD-S05 §32 and §32.1 physical PDF pp.53–54. Source-derived facts: defined response/recall process, traceability/effectiveness and serious-event authority escalation. Original record/communication advice is not a numerical deadline or recall classification. Scope: food manufacture and related distribution facts verified; separate medical recall guidance excluded.

## GUD-24 — Turn a food hygiene checklist into useful evidence

### Customer body starts

Start with the food activity and process being checked. A receiving area, preparation bench, production line and delivery vehicle do not need identical observations. NAFDAC's food hygiene guide supplies a general framework while recognising more specific sector requirements. Select the applicable controls before treating a checklist result as meaningful.

For each item, write the criterion, what was actually observed, where and when it was checked, and who recorded it. Use distinct outcomes for acceptable against the stated criterion, action needed, not checked and not applicable with a reason. An empty box or an unavailable record is not evidence that the condition was satisfactory.

Keep technical limits tied to the relevant product and validated procedure. A temperature, time, concentration or other limit copied from an unrelated recipe can create false reassurance. Record the unit, measurement method, instrument reference and actual result. Where the limit or method is unresolved, raise the gap with the person responsible instead of inventing a passing value.

Link each concern to an action owner and evidence of follow-up. Separate correcting the working area from deciding what happens to food that may already have been affected. Cleaning a surface does not establish the suitability of an earlier batch. Never taste suspect food to check its safety.

Avoid unnecessary medical details in a general checklist. Use the appropriate restricted route for staff-health matters. A completed checklist records that particular review; it is not a certificate of safety, a full hazard-control system or an automatic permission to release a product.

### Customer body ends

**Internal claim map:** PROD-S06 §2.1 physical PDF p.8 (baseline/sector scope), §5.2.1 pp.18–19 (process-specific time/temperature control), §§6–7 pp.22–25 (hygiene/people context). Original observation/status and follow-up method; no numerical safe limit, diagnostic recommendation or reproduction of source forms. Source commercial-reproduction limitation must remain in actual rights review.

## GUD-25 — Keep suspect regulated stock controlled while arranging disposal

### Customer body starts

Damaged, expired or otherwise suspect regulated stock needs a documented decision. Removing an item from a sales list does not establish that it has been safely disposed of. NAFDAC's disposal guide provides an Agency process involving an inventory, verification and supervised destruction arrangements for the regulated products within its scope.

Start by identifying the product, category, batch, quantity, unit, location and reason it is being held. Record what is known and what still needs assessment. Keep the item under the site's authorised control arrangements and prevent it being confused with saleable stock. Do not open, mix, drain, burn or repackage suspect material to make an inventory easier; obtain appropriate instructions for the actual hazard.

Assign a person to verify the applicable NAFDAC route and any other relevant environmental or sector requirements. Keep the enquiry, instructions, application reference and inventory version together. Medicine-specific professional roles or handling categories must not be copied automatically into an unrelated food or cosmetics operation.

Distinguish proposed disposal, authorised arrangements, transfer of custody and disposal actually witnessed. Record the real quantities at each step, the responsible parties and the evidence received. A transport receipt is not itself a destruction certificate, and a blank certificate template is not proof that disposal occurred.

Do not assume that a refund, supplier credit or insurance settlement resolves the physical stock. This guidance does not choose a disposal method, quote a fee, promise a service date or authorise donation or resale. Use the actual instructions and keep unresolved quantities visible until they are properly accounted for.

### Customer body ends

**Internal claim map:** PROD-S07 §§1–3 physical PDF pp.2–3 (scope/inventory/application), §§4–8 pp.4–5 (verification, supervised process, evidence/certificate). Original custody/status distinction and unsafe-handling avoidance. No pharmacy staffing requirement or waste-treatment technique applied beyond verified product scope. No copying of its inventory annex or prescription of medicine sorting.

## GUD-26 — Check a food advertisement before committing to publication

### Customer body starts

Keep the advertisement review separate from product registration. NAFDAC's March 2026 food advertising guide covers registered food products and identifies advertising materials and supporting evidence for its process. The local food registration guide also states that product registration does not automatically confer advertisement permission.

Prepare a campaign record before approving expenditure or publishing a claim. Identify the exact product, intended audience, medium, language, artwork or script version and proposed publication period. Include online material as well as print or broadcast. Save the version actually submitted so a later edit is not mistaken for the material considered earlier.

Make a claim list alongside the creative work. For each factual statement, identify its supporting evidence and check that the wording does not go beyond it. A testimonial, influencer script or attractive graphic can still communicate a factual claim. Do not invent health benefits, official endorsement, approvals or customer experiences to complete an advertisement.

Confirm the applicable approval route and any product-specific restrictions through the official source. The guide identifies prohibited advertising categories, including breast-milk substitutes and infant or young children’s food; this workflow is not an approval route around a prohibition. Alcohol and other sensitive categories also need their particular rules checked. Where an agent or featured person is involved, retain the actual authority and agreements relevant to their role.

Keep submission, queries, revised material and the official outcome distinct. Check the scope and conditions of any permission before using it for a different product, claim or channel. This entry supplies no approval guarantee, fee or universal processing deadline, and it does not establish compliance with every other advertising or consumer-protection requirement.
