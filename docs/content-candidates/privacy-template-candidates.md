## TPL-31 — Data processing agreement

Premium · Download · Controller-to-processor service relationship. A joint-controller arrangement or independent-controller disclosure must not be forced into this form. This form uses explicit agreed operational times rather than asserting that a sample number is a statutory deadline. Customer needs a completed processing schedule, security schedule and subprocessor list.

### CUSTOMER BODY START

# Data processing agreement

Agreement reference: **[reference]** · Effective date: **[date]**

**Controller:** [legal name, address, authorised contact]  
**Processor:** [legal name, address, authorised contact]  
Related service agreement: [title, reference and date]

### 1. Scope and instructions

This agreement governs the Processor's handling of personal data on the Controller's behalf for the service described in Schedule A. The Controller determines the relevant purposes and documents lawful instructions. The Processor processes that data only on those instructions, including approved instructions about access, disclosure, deletion and transfers, unless applicable law requires otherwise. Where lawful, the Processor informs the Controller of that requirement before acting.

If an instruction appears inconsistent with applicable data-protection requirements, the Processor promptly alerts the Controller, explains the concern, and pauses the affected action while the parties resolve it where a pause is lawful. Neither party treats this agreement as permission to use service data for unrelated advertising, sale, general model training or its own new purposes.

### 2. Controller responsibilities

The Controller identifies the applicable lawful basis and any additional conditions, gives the required information to individuals, and supplies only data necessary for the agreed purpose. It gives instructions through [authorised channel] and keeps the instruction and authority record current. The Controller assesses whether a high-risk activity requires an impact assessment or other additional measure. The Processor supplies relevant information and assistance; this agreement does not itself complete that assessment.

### 3. People and security

The Processor limits access to authorised people who need it for the service and are bound to protect confidentiality. It maintains the specific technical and organisational measures in Schedule B and reviews whether they remain proportionate to the data and risks. It must not materially reduce the agreed protection without informing the Controller and resolving the effect on the service.

The Processor separates customers' data appropriately, manages access changes, records relevant security events, and protects data during transfer, storage, restoration and disposal as described in Schedule B. The parties do not describe an unimplemented control or unverified certification as being in place.

### 4. Other processors

Approved subprocessors and their roles, locations and safeguards are listed in Schedule C. Before engaging a new subprocessor, the Processor notifies the Controller through [channel] with enough information to assess the change and follows [specific approval/objection process and agreed period]. Subcontracting requires written terms appropriate to these obligations. If a reasonable protection concern cannot be resolved, the parties agree a safe alternative or the affected service ends under the service agreement; notification alone does not override an agreed restriction.

### 5. Individuals' requests and assessments

The Processor promptly forwards a request concerning the Controller's data to [contact], unless instructed otherwise or legally required to respond directly. It provides reasonable assistance to locate, access, correct, restrict, export or delete relevant data and to support lawful objections, investigations and assessments. ### 6. Security incidents

On becoming aware of a personal-data breach affecting the service, the Processor notifies the Controller using the agreed escalation channel [channel]. Initial information may be supplemented as the investigation develops.

The notification includes, so far as known, what happened, the categories and approximate numbers of affected people and records, likely consequences, containment and proposed remedies, contact details and outstanding uncertainties. The Processor preserves appropriate evidence, keeps the required breach record, responds to the Controller's information requests, takes reasonable containment measures, and assists the Controller's risk assessment and legally required notifications. It must not delay the initial warning merely to complete a final investigation. Neither party falsely reports an unconfirmed event as a completed finding.

### 7. Locations and transfers

Processing and remote-access locations are [list]. A proposed new location or international transfer requires the agreed instruction and a verified applicable transfer arrangement recorded in Schedule C or an addendum. This agreement alone is not a finding of adequacy or permission to transfer to any country. Relevant onward transfers and subcontractor access must be included in the assessment.

### 8. Records and assurance

The Processor supplies information reasonably needed to demonstrate its performance of these obligations. The Controller may assess the agreed controls through proportionate documentation review and, where reasonably necessary, an appropriately scoped inspection or independent assessment under [notice, confidentiality, security and cost arrangements]. These arrangements must not prevent information reasonably required by law or lawful regulator access. Reports must distinguish tested controls from planned controls.

### 9. Ending processing

At the end of the service, or on a lawful instruction, the Processor returns or deletes the specified personal data according to the Controller's choice and the agreed schedule [schedule]. Backups follow [actual expiry and restore controls]; deleted data must not silently return to active use following restoration. The Processor provides [defined completion evidence].

The parties keep authorised contacts, schedules and instructions current. Amendments are recorded in writing by authorised representatives. Where the service agreement conflicts with this agreement on personal-data protection, this agreement prevails to the extent necessary for that subject, subject to applicable law. Schedule D records the parties' due diligence, risk assessment and agreed indemnity, insurance, force-majeure and dispute provisions, either in full or through precise incorporated clauses supplied with this agreement. ## TPL-32 — Data retention policy

Premium · Download · Internal policy with a complete business-specific schedule required. Does not supply a fixed number of years for all data. Tax, employment, sector, litigation and contract requirements need separately verified current sources and applicability.

### CUSTOMER BODY START

# Data retention and disposal policy

Organisation: **[legal name]** · Owner: **[role/contact]**  
Version: **[version]** · Effective date: **[date]** · Next review: **[date]**

### Purpose and scope

We retain personal information for identified, lawful purposes and dispose of it when it is no longer needed for those purposes, subject to specific applicable retention requirements. This policy covers [systems, paper records, devices, backups, processors and business functions]. It applies to staff and service providers with responsibility for those records.

### Responsibilities

The policy owner maintains the schedule and coordinates changes. Each record owner identifies the relevant purpose, source, location and retention requirement and checks that the period remains justified. System owners operate deletion, restricted archive and restore controls. Authorised decision-makers record legal holds and their release. All staff report unnecessary copies, expired records or disposal failures through [channel].

### Retention schedule

Every record category must have a defined trigger and duration before routine retention is approved. A period begins from the stated event, not automatically from the day someone opens the file. “Keep indefinitely” and “as long as needed” are not completed schedule entries.

| Record category and system | Purpose and lawful basis | Period and start trigger | Verified obligation or business justification | Owner | End action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Category/location] | [Specific purpose/basis] | [Period from defined event] | [Applicable source or documented necessity; review date] | [Role] | [Delete/anonymise/restricted retention] |

Different purposes may require different periods. Where one record has an ongoing lawful purpose after its original use ends, the owner records that purpose, limits access and use to it, and sets the next end or review trigger. Do not apply a tax or employment period to unrelated marketing, identity or support records merely because they concern the same person.

### Collection, copies and access

Collect only information required for the stated purpose. Store it in the approved location and avoid duplicate exports, personal inboxes and uncontrolled device copies. The schedule covers attachments and generated files as well as database entries. Restrict access when a record moves from active use to an archive and record the reason for continued retention.

### Routine disposal

The system owner produces a list of records due for disposal at [actual operating interval]. Before disposal, check valid holds, outstanding requests and dependencies. Use the approved method appropriate to the medium: [actual digital deletion/anonymisation and paper disposal methods]. Anonymisation must prevent reasonably foreseeable re-identification; removing a name while retaining a linkable identifier is not automatically anonymisation.

Record the disposal batch, categories, period covered, method, authorisation, execution date and outcome. Keep the disposal log limited to evidence needed to demonstrate the action; do not recreate the deleted personal data in that log. Failures are retried or escalated to [owner] with a defined next action.

### Holds and exceptions

A specific legal, regulatory or dispute-preservation requirement may temporarily prevent disposal. The authorised owner records the reason, affected records, access restriction, start date, evidence and next review date. A hold applies only to relevant records and does not authorise unrelated use. When the reason ends, the owner releases the hold and resumes the appropriate schedule. An employee's preference to keep a copy is not a sufficient exception.

### Rights requests and erasure

Requests are routed to [rights channel] and checked proportionately. The owner determines what can be erased, what must be retained for an identified lawful reason, and what restriction is appropriate while a dispute or request is resolved. Explain a valid retention exception and next step to the requester. An erasure request is not automatically rejected because a backup exists.

### Processors, backups and restoration

Provider agreements identify retention, return, deletion, backup expiry and proof of completion. The actual backup cycle is [verified cycle and maximum expiry]. Access to backups is limited to recovery and other specifically authorised purposes. Where immediate selective removal is not feasible, explain the actual restriction and expiry arrangement, assess its lawfulness, and prevent routine use of the retained copy.

Maintain sufficient deletion/hold instructions so that restoration does not silently reactivate information already deleted or restricted. After a restore, apply those instructions before ordinary access resumes and record the check. Test provider deletion and restoration arrangements rather than assuming that a policy statement proves them.

### Incidents, review and change

Report retention beyond its justified period, unsuccessful disposal, uncontrolled copies and accidental destruction through [incident channel]. Preserve proportionate evidence while containing the issue. The owner reviews the schedule on [date/interval] and after material changes to services, law, providers or processing. Changes record who approved them, the reason and how existing records are affected. Notify affected people or update the privacy notice where required by the change.

Validation: all systems/record categories have a period and trigger; any statutory period is source-verified and applicable; backup limits agree with actual contracts; restored deletions are tested; policy approval is a customer's business decision, not AI certification.
