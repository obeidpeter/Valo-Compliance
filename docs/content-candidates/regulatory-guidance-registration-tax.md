## GUD-01 — Check the name you trade under

### Customer body

Business-name registration depends on the operator and the name being used. Section 814 of the Companies and Allied Matters Act applies to an individual, firm or corporation with a place of business in Nigeria carrying on business under a name. It does not simply say that every small business must become a limited company.

For an individual, compare the trading name with the person's true surname, allowing true forenames or their initials. For a firm, check the true surnames of all partners, allowing the individual partners' true forenames or initials. For a company, compare the trading name with its corporate name without additions. A different trading brand can matter even when the company is already incorporated. The Act includes patronymics within surnames and recognised forename abbreviations within initials; spelling alone should not decide the result.

Three qualifications need checking. Wording that merely identifies succession to a former owner does not itself trigger registration. Where two or more individual partners share a surname, adding an “s” does not itself trigger it. The section also provides an exception when a court-appointed receiver or manager carries on the business. Ordinary management arrangements do not establish that exception.

As a practical preparation step, write down the name actually used, the operator's legal form and the relevant true names or corporate name. Record any claimed qualification and its facts. Then check the [CAC business-name route](https://www.cac.gov.ng/services/business-name). A result of “Not indicated by these answers” concerns this naming check only; it does not establish general exemption from registration, approval of the name or compliance with other obligations.

## GUD-02 — Identify whether you have a company or a registered business name

### Customer body

“Registered business” can describe different arrangements. Before completing a contract, tax form or supplier application, identify what CAC actually registered and who is carrying on the business. A familiar trading name is not enough to answer those questions.

Under section 42 of the Companies and Allied Matters Act, incorporation creates a body corporate under the name in the company's memorandum, from the date in its incorporation certificate. Section 41 requires the certificate to identify whether the company is private or public and whether members' liability is limited by shares, limited by guarantee or unlimited. Section 21 distinguishes those liability structures. Do not assume that every company offers the same liability arrangement, or that incorporation removes every possible personal responsibility.

Business-name registration operates differently. Sections 814 and 817 deal with the business name of an individual, firm or corporation and the certificate issued for that name. A company can itself use a separate business name. A business-name certificate alone therefore does not establish that the named business was incorporated as a company or identify a limited-liability structure.

For practical recordkeeping, keep a short identity sheet beside the registration documents: registered operator, legal form, exact corporate name if any, trading name, registration reference and certificate date. This sheet is an organisational aid, not a substitute for the official record. Use the [CAC portal](https://icrp.cac.gov.ng/) to check the relevant registration service or obtain help correcting a mismatch.

If you cannot establish the operator or registration type, pause before describing the business as a limited company in a document. This guide helps identify existing records; it does not choose the best structure for your circumstances, assess incorporation eligibility or determine your tax treatment.

## GUD-03 — Keep the business-name certificate available at your premises

### Customer body

A business-name registration certificate is more than a file to save after registration. Section 817 of the Companies and Allied Matters Act addresses how the certificate is issued and displayed by the individual, firm or corporation registered under the business-name provisions.

The section requires the certificate to be exhibited and maintained in a conspicuous position at the principal place of the registered business. If the business has more than one place of business, it specifies the original certificate at the principal place and a copy certified by the Registrar at each other place. An ordinary photocopy should not automatically be described as the certified copy mentioned in the Act.

There are practical exceptions and changes to account for. If CAC issues a fresh certificate after a registered change, the display requirement applies to that fresh certificate. Where a certificate is lost, destroyed or illegible, the section allows a copy certified by the Registrar to be displayed instead. Keep those circumstances separate from simply choosing to print an unverified document.

CAC's current business-name page describes electronic certificates and certified registration extracts. If your certificate was issued electronically, confirm with CAC which current official document or certified copy meets the display requirement for your premises; this guide does not assume that a phone screenshot satisfies it.

As an optional check, list your principal and other business locations, identify the certificate version held at each, and record any replacement needed. Use the [CAC portal](https://icrp.cac.gov.ng/) for the official document or support route. This entry concerns registered business names, not a universal certificate-display rule for every company, permit or online activity, and it does not quote a penalty amount.

## GUD-04 — Update changed business-name particulars

### Customer body

After registering a business name, keep the registered particulars aligned with the business. Section 818 of the Companies and Allied Matters Act applies when a particular required under section 815 changes. It expressly excludes a change in an individual's age from this notification rule.

Section 815's particulars include the business name, the general nature of the business and the addresses of the principal and other places of business. Depending on the operator, they also include particulars of individuals, partners or a company. A move, a change in the business name or a change in the recorded operator details should therefore prompt a check of the registration record rather than only an update to your website or invoices.

The statute requires notification to the Registrar within 28 days after the relevant change. The notice must be in writing and signed in the manner section 815 provides. This is the statutory interval, not a calculation of your personal deadline: establish the actual change, its date and the appropriate signer before setting a reminder. Do not substitute an assumed date or treat every ordinary birthday as a filing event.

As an optional working method, keep a change log showing the old information, the new information, when the change occurred and the supporting document. Check the [CAC portal](https://icrp.cac.gov.ng/) for the current change-registration process and retain the submission acknowledgement and updated record. Section 817 permits CAC to amend the previous certificate or issue a new one after a registered change.

This entry concerns the registered particulars of a business name. A company changing its own corporate particulars, a business ceasing operations, and changes to tax records can involve different procedures; completing one update should not be treated as proof that every register has been updated.

## GUD-05 — Prepare the annual return for a registered business name

### Customer body

Running a business under a registered business name involves continuing records, not just the original certificate. Section 822 of the Companies and Allied Matters Act requires an individual, firm or corporation carrying on business under that registered name to deliver an annual return to CAC. The calendar year in which the business name is registered is expressly excluded.

The return describes the operator and the nature of the business. It must be accompanied by a financial statement covering the preceding January-to-December period. The section specifies who signs: the individuals for an individual or an all-individual firm, and a director and secretary for a corporation or corporate partner. Do not assume that one unnamed staff member can sign every kind of operator's return.

Section 822 states 30 June as the annual filing date for the years it covers. Check the registration year, the reporting period, the operator and the current CAC filing route before deciding what is due. This is a business-name registry return; paying tax or submitting a company's tax return does not itself supply the return described here.

Prepare the registration particulars and the relevant financial statement, then use the [CAC portal](https://icrp.cac.gov.ng/). Its notice checked on 14 November 2026 says annual-return filing is available for business names registered before July 2025 while a system upgrade is in progress. If your registration is newer or the service is unavailable, ask CAC for the correct submission route. A portal limitation should not be assumed to create a legal extension.

An optional filing folder can hold the reporting period, submitted version, authorised signatures, acknowledgement and any CAC response. This guide does not decide late-filing penalties, whether a business has legally ceased, or how outstanding historic years should be regularised.

## GUD-06 — Keep company annual returns separate from tax filings

### Customer body

A company's annual return to CAC is a corporate-registry filing. Section 417 of the Companies and Allied Matters Act establishes the annual-return duty, while sections 418 to 420 distinguish the applicable forms for different company categories. This filing should not be confused with the company's income-tax return to the revenue authority.

The Act contains exceptions and category-specific requirements. Section 417 excludes the incorporation year and, where its stated condition concerning an annual general meeting is met, the following year. Section 237 contains its own meeting exceptions. Do not turn an exemption from holding a meeting into a permanent exemption from annual returns, or assume that a business calling itself “small” has established the Act's small-company classification.

The return's particulars and attachments depend on the company type. Sections 422 and 424 address accompanying documents and exceptions, and section 423 requires particular certificates for private and small companies. A complete filing therefore needs more than a registration number and a payment receipt. Verify the company's legal category and the documents appropriate to it.

Section 421 links timing to the annual general meeting and expressly does not apply to a company with only one member. This guide does not assign one universal deadline to every company. Confirm the incorporation date, return year, membership, meeting facts and any applicable extension with CAC before setting a date.

As an optional preparation step, keep a register of return years, the current corporate particulars, supporting financial documents, responsible signers and acknowledgements. Use the [CAC portal](https://icrp.cac.gov.ng/) for the current filing route. The distinction between this filing and tax returns can help prevent one successful submission being mistaken for completion of both obligations.

## GUD-07 — Keep company accounting records that explain the transactions

### Customer body

If the business is a company, section 374 of the Companies and Allied Matters Act requires accounting records sufficient to explain its transactions and show its financial position with reasonable accuracy. The records must also support the directors in preparing financial statements that meet the Act's requirements. A list of bank balances alone may not explain what the company received, spent, owns or owes.

The section specifically calls for day-to-day entries of money received and spent, the matters to which those entries relate, and a record of assets and liabilities. A company dealing in goods also needs year-end stock statements and the underlying stocktaking records. Its rule on detailed records of goods bought and sold includes an exception for goods sold in ordinary retail trade; that exception does not erase the wider accounting-record duty.

Section 375 requires the records to be kept at the registered office or another place in Nigeria chosen by the directors, and to be open to inspection by company officers. Subject to directions under the relevant winding-up rules, the records must be preserved for six years from when they were made. The section also addresses electronic copies in addition to original hard copies and requires attention to the quality and security of the technology used.

As optional organisation, link each transaction to its invoice, receipt or other explanation, reconcile bank activity, maintain an asset-and-liability list, and keep stocktaking evidence where relevant. Restrict editing access and maintain recoverable copies.

These are company-law records. Tax records can have a different retention starting point, so do not automatically delete a document when one clock expires. Use the [CAC official site](https://www.cac.gov.ng/) for company-record queries and consult the separate tax-record guidance for the tax requirement.

## GUD-08 — Retrieve the correct Tax ID before creating another record

### Customer body

A Tax ID identifies a person for tax administration; it is not a certificate that every tax obligation has been completed. Section 4 of the Nigeria Tax Administration Act requires a taxable person to register with the relevant tax authority and obtain a Tax ID. Which person and authority are involved depends on the actual operator and tax circumstances, not simply the trading name on a shop sign.

Section 7 contains useful safeguards. A person with a valid Tax ID must not apply for or be issued another one. Multiple IDs should be reported to the relevant tax authority for unification, and one person's ID cannot be transferred to or used by another person. A change of trading name should therefore not be treated automatically as a reason to create an unrelated new taxpayer identity.

The current [NRS/JRB Tax ID portal](https://taxid.nrs.gov.ng/) provides retrieval using existing identity information. Its explanation distinguishes retrieval of an existing identity from a fresh registration exercise. It uses NIN details for individuals and CAC registration information for businesses, with the further checks shown by the official service. Enter sensitive identity information directly on the official service rather than placing it in a general business-document draft.

As optional preparation, establish the legal operator, check whether an ID already exists, and compare the returned identity with the official registration details. If the record is missing, duplicated, inactive or mismatched, contact the relevant tax authority through its official route; do not invent a number or borrow another business's ID.

This entry concerns current domestic tax administration. It does not decide whether every reader is a taxable person, the correct treatment of non-residents, the amount of tax due, or whether a past tax filing was valid.

## GUD-09 — Prepare a Nigerian company's income-tax return even when no tax is payable

### Customer body

For a positively identified Nigerian company, do not decide whether to prepare an annual income-tax return solely by looking at the amount of tax payable. Section 11 of the Nigeria Tax Administration Act requires a company to file a self-assessment return at least annually, with or without a notice from the Service, whether or not it is liable to pay tax. A zero-tax calculation does not by itself remove that filing requirement.

The return includes the prescribed self-assessment information, financial statements and tax computations, payment evidence where tax is due, and a principal officer's attestation. The section contains a small-company alternative concerning the statement of accounts in place of audited financial statements. This guide does not decide whether your company qualifies for that alternative; a descriptive label such as “small business” is insufficient.

Section 11 also requires returns for the cessation year and outstanding returns when a company permanently ceases operations. Closing a shop or stopping sales should not automatically remove the filing item from your records. The Act requires a designated representative for tax matters; if that representative is a paid agent, the applicable accreditation requirement matters. Section 33 also permits taxpayers to represent themselves.

As optional preparation, gather the incorporation record, Tax ID, accounting period, financial records, previous filings and any cessation information. Confirm the current submission route with the [Nigeria Revenue Service](https://www.nrs.gov.ng/) and preserve the filed version and acknowledgement.

This entry does not calculate a deadline, tax rate or liability. Timing depends on company and accounting-period facts, and older or transitional periods need separate treatment. Non-resident companies have additional provisions and exceptions. A CAC annual return is a different filing, and completing it does not supply this income-tax return.

## GUD-10 — Keep tax records with the correct retention starting point

### Customer body

Tax records are needed to explain the transactions behind a return, including when the final tax payable is zero. Section 31 of the Nigeria Tax Administration Act requires books or records of account whether or not a person is liable to pay tax. The records must contain enough information about relevant transactions to establish the person's tax liability. Do not treat a belief that the business owes no tax as a reason to discard its records.

The section requires the records to be in English and consistent with any format prescribed by the relevant tax authority. Where records are maintained in another language, the authority can demand an English translation, at the person's expense, certified by a sworn translator. An informal machine translation should not be represented as that certified translation.

Section 31 states a minimum retention period of six years after the year of assessment to which the income relates. That is a different starting point from the company-law requirement measured from when a company accounting record was made. Do not combine the two into a single automatic deletion date. Historical periods, other applicable retention duties and an active query or dispute need their own checks before disposal.

As optional practice, organise supporting documents by relevant period and transaction: sales, purchases, receipts, expenses, assets, liabilities and the explanations linking them to the accounts. Preserve the original information, track corrections and keep a recoverable copy. These categories are a practical filing method, not an exhaustive statutory list of every business's records.

Ask the relevant tax authority through its official service which prescribed record format applies to your situation; the [NRS official site](https://www.nrs.gov.ng/) is a starting route for federal company-tax matters. This guide does not certify a tax calculation, prescribe software or authorise destruction of particular records.
