01 / DECISION SUMMARY
Executive roadmap and planning basis
Build Valo as a commercial platform with documents and compliance guidance as equal pillars. This revision adopts AI drafting, separate AI checks and automated content publication because Nigerian legal, tax and sector specialists are unavailable. No per-item specialist or human content sign-off is assumed. Valo’s product owner remains accountable for scope, operating policy and platform release.
RECOMMENDED DELIVERY BASELINE  Plan R1 over 20 elapsed weeks from an approved, staffed kickoff, followed by an eight-week R2 growth window and a gated R3 expansion horizon. These are planning estimates, not a guaranteed delivery date or a commitment to start development automatically.

What customers receive at R1
R1 targets a public website; 40 AI-reviewed templates (12 free, 28 premium); 12 guided editors; four customer AI drafting types; secure workspaces; free source-linked guidance and checker; real purchases/exports; reminders; enquiries; and an internal AI editorial pipeline with automated publishing and correction controls.
All states/FCT and 20 industries are selectable. Six deeper packs and Lagos/FCT/Bayelsa receive initial source-check priority. Labels distinguish AI-reviewed content, evidence dates and coverage gaps. Selectability does not imply exhaustive local coverage; AI review is not professional verification.
Assumptions behind the schedule
The plan assumes two full-time engineers, part-time design/QA, an active product owner and a content operations lead. AI supplies legal, tax and sector drafting/review capacity; specialist recruitment is not on the critical path. Authoritative sources, rights evidence, model access/budget, security assessment and operations must be ready early. Holidays and prolonged procurement remain outside the baseline.
How to use the two documents
Use VALO-TRD-001 version 1.1 as the acceptance contract, including EDP-001–012 for internal AI content operations. Use this roadmap for sequence, capacity and gates. The four customer AI drafting tools remain a separate feature. Convert requirements into testable tickets; changes to Must scope require a recorded decision.
No hidden commitments
Prices, budget, hiring and launch date remain unapproved; no existing code audit is implied. Nigerian specialist availability is not assumed. Optional future professional review may improve coverage but is not a prerequisite for AI publication. Real provider services remain gated on actual provider availability.
Document control: VALO-ROADMAP-001 • Version 1.1 • Revised 13 November 2026 • AI editorial baseline. Scope: Valo documents and compliance, not Valo Pay or an earlier lending product. Specialist pre-publication sign-off is replaced throughout this plan.

Five principles for delivery decisions
Complete a journey before expanding the menu. Prioritise working document and purchase flows.
Treat content as maintained work. Fund source access, AI checks, rights evidence and correction handling.
Keep guidance free and uncertainty visible. Never make a template purchase appear legally compulsory.
Release on evidence. Real storage, payments, recovery, support and passing publication gates are required.
Expand by demand and readiness. Add scope only when source coverage and operating capacity support it.
RELEASE LABELS  R1 = production commercial core. R2 = growth, collaboration and provider coordination. R3 = selectively activated marketplace and deeper sector/integration capabilities. Every feature must clearly identify its release rather than appearing half-built at launch.

Capacity interpretation
FTE is human full-time equivalent capacity. Removing the 0.3 FTE specialist-review allowance changes the baseline from about 85 to about 79 person-weeks, including 40 engineering person-weeks. AI review is usage-based expenditure, not human FTE. Measure retry/hold rates and source acquisition time in W1–2 before committing the calendar.
One person may hold compatible business/content roles. AI drafting, reviewing and publishing remain separate service roles and run records, not fictional people. A solo founder with coding agents must re-estimate engineering, security assessment, support and content operations capacity; this revision removes only the unavailable legal/tax/sector review dependency.
Decision and escalation cadence
Hold weekly product/engineering/content reviews, twice-weekly blocker reviews during integration and gate reviews at phase ends. Content operations monitors sources, held items and corrections; unresolved legal questions stay held or are narrowed and rechecked. Product owns scope/spend. These meetings do not approve individual content items or certify legal interpretations.
If capacity is smaller, preserve payment/security/privacy controls and AI publication gates; defer expansion. Source gaps or unavailable AI service must create a visible hold or coverage gap, not an unsupported published item.

Critical path

Non-negotiable launch blockers
Cross-tenant leakage, incorrect entitlements, exposed secrets, silent draft loss, unsupported material legal claims, false professional-review labels, bypassed publication gates, high/critical exploitable defects, missing template rights or failed restore block launch. A polished demo or model confidence score cannot waive these conditions.
Meaning of “done”
Every R1 Must item has an owner, revision and passing implementation evidence. Content also has source/rights evidence, separate AI findings, test results, public disclosure and a release manifest. Required manual security/usability checks remain. Blocked capabilities are disabled or unavailable, never presented as live.
Security testing follows a mapped OWASP ASVS baseline; accessibility aims at WCAG 2.2 AA with manual review as well as automated checks. Neither a vulnerability scan nor an accessibility score alone is sufficient evidence. [S19–S20]
Record operational sign-offs, AI publication manifests and residual risks in the release register. The product owner may accept non-critical scope changes, but cannot waive failed mandatory evidence gates, data protection, payment integrity or truthful content status.

09 / MEASURE USEFUL OUTCOMES
Validation plan and product metrics
Beta design
Target 30 beta SMEs across the six deeper sectors, varied sizes and digital confidence. Include at least five outside Lagos/FCT/Bayelsa to test coverage gaps. Begin with a smaller invited cohort. Disclose AI drafting/review and absence of specialist content review; increase access only as support and reliability stabilise.
Ask each participant to find a relevant document, use or customise it, export it, run the requirements checker and explain a recommended next step in their own words. Test paid flows only with consent and clear refund arrangements. Do not require users to upload sensitive evidence to complete beta tasks.
METRIC | PROPOSED TARGET / DECISION RULE | DEFINITION
Usability task success | ≥80% complete the primary assigned journey without facilitator intervention. | Successful users ÷ users attempting the same defined task; report device and task type.
Document reliability | ≥95% beta export jobs complete within one retry; no silent content/revision corruption. | Successful job count ÷ attempted jobs; separate user validation failures from system failures.
Checker quality | 100% critical fixture outcomes correct; users can understand reasons and gaps. | 80-scenario test suite plus qualitative explanation checks; do not use a misleading “legal accuracy” percentage.
Payment
integrity | 100% beta transactions reconciled; zero duplicate grants or incorrect amount/currency acceptance. | PSP records compared with internal orders, entitlements, refunds and receipts.
Customer AI quality | ≥95% on the 60-case rubric and zero critical privacy/false-legal-claim failures. | 60 fixed cases, source/rubric-backed expectations and versioned model/prompt; separate from editorial testing.
Content readiness | 100% of published items have sources/rights as applicable, AI disclosure, complete manifests and current checks; zero critical false releases in 60 editorial cases. | Eligible published items ÷ published items; ≥95% editorial-suite pass, all critical cases pass; gaps/held items excluded from coverage.
Operational readiness | Restore/incident drills pass; all critical alerts have a named owner. | Evidence in release pack, not a verbal assurance.

Post-launch learning dashboard
Track activation, first-use time, discovery-to-use, purchase conversion, return use, support, refunds and cost per completed document. Add source coverage, editorial holds/retries, correction frequency, stale-item exposure and time to suspend/correct. Record denominators, risk class and sample size; AI pass rates do not measure certified legal accuracy.
Numeric thresholds here are proposed internal hypotheses, not published industry benchmarks. Commercial conversion and retention targets should be agreed after the first meaningful cohort baseline rather than invented as guaranteed forecasts.

Paid-booking gate: what must exist first
Define who contracts with the customer, who receives payment, the scope and deliverables, scheduling/acceptance states, cancellation/refund rights, complaints, disputes, provider responsibility and any referral fee. Decide the compliant PSP settlement model; do not add a Valo wallet, escrow or client-money holding arrangement by default.
Run a small controlled pilot only after actual provider acceptance and verified credentials. Keep each booking’s statement of work, communications, payment status and resolution history auditable. Valo must not guarantee licence approval, filing acceptance or professional outcomes.
Expansion prioritisation
Rank expansion by customer need, authoritative source availability, AI gate performance, maintenance capacity, effort and risk. Prefer useful depth over catalogue size. Healthcare, finance and energy require activity-specific evidence and enhanced checks; unresolved content remains unavailable. Optional later specialist review can deepen coverage, without becoming a blanket publication dependency.
R2/R3 quantities and windows are provisional. Review them after the first post-launch cohort and approve a new delivery baseline before committing expenditure or public dates.

11 / PROTECT THE CORE PRODUCT
Risk register and contingency rules
RISK / SIGNAL | MITIGATION | OWNER / ESCALATION
Scope expansion / repeated “small” additions | Lock R1 Must scope; require value, effort and displaced work for each change; defer low-evidence features. | Product owner; review weekly.
Unsupported or conflicting AI content | Retrieve authoritative text; separate AI checks; deterministic gates; hold unresolved claims; source/rights evidence and honest disclosure. | Content owner + pipeline; narrow scope or defer affected items when evidence fails.
Nationwide coverage overstatement | Show scope/gaps on every affected page and result; distinguish federal, state/FCT and LGA layers. | Product + content; misleading claim is a release blocker.
Replit worker/migration/backup mismatch | Complete proof in W1–2; use replaceable adapters and approved alternate infrastructure when necessary. | Technical lead; architecture gate blocks further coupling.
Payment race, mismatch or provider delay | Atomic fulfilment, raw-signature verification, sandbox failure cases and daily reconciliation. | Engineering + finance; disable checkout for integrity incidents.
Cross-tenant data or private-file exposure | Server enforcement, least privilege, negative tests and independent assessment. | Security lead; stop beta/public access until contained.
AI unreliable, slow or too expensive | Limit task scope; evaluation, quotas, budgets and kill switch; manual editor remains available. | Technical lead; disable AI independently if needed.
Poor demand or weak conversion | Interview users, instrument full journeys and test useful template bundles; avoid premature marketplace investment. | Product owner; re-prioritise R2 on cohort evidence.
Provider quality or enquiry mishandling | List only checked providers, retain sharing consent and track responses; no fake booking confirmation. | Service operations; remove unready providers.
Under-resourced support/content maintenance | Fund named owners and schedule recurring review; publish only the coverage that can be maintained. | Product owner; delay growth rather than accumulate unsafe content.

Contingency policy
Keep the 20-week baseline subject to measured delivery and AI pipeline throughput. Retain the proposed 20% financial effort reserve. Re-estimate delays caused by missing sources, review failures, model changes, procurement or holidays; do not hide them in late sprints. Unavailable legal/tax/sector reviewers are no longer a schedule dependency.
If constrained, defer provider payments, subscriptions, extra sectors, collaboration and advanced AI first. Preserve isolation, payment checks, rights evidence, AI publishing gates, backups, privacy controls and honest communication. Neither a headcount shortfall nor an inventory target justifies gate bypass.
