For GUD-08, the Joint Revenue Board’s Tax ID retrieval page at taxid.jrb.gov.ng/how-it-works may supply the portal instructions. For GUD-27 through GUD-30, the Federal Ministry of Labour’s Labour Act at nelex.gov.ng/documents/LABOUR_ACT.pdf may confirm the cited sections alongside Valo’s registered copy. Each release requires the article’s existing claim checks and owner sign-off.

The product owner approved this rule on 22 November 2026. A changed article, portal explanation or Labour Act edition needs fresh evidence. The original reviewed guidance policy keeps its approved digest.

## Employment

The Ministry's National Electronic Labour Exchange links to its Labour Act and Trade Unions Act. The five complete passages cited in the Investment Promotion Commission’s copy occur verbatim in the Ministry’s Labour Act. Each of the 22 distinct quotations counted across the four guides matched. A separately held printed copy of Cap. L1, Issue 1 supplied the correct section and paragraph numbering. | Guide | Printed Cap. L1 pages | Scope checked |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GUD-27 | 7–8, 54 | Section 7 covers the particulars, incorporation of accessible documents and the qualifying written contract. Section 91 limits who is a worker. |
| GUD-28 | 5–6, 54 | Sections 5(1), (5) and (7) cover the basis for deductions, the officer’s written consent for the specified loss, the overpayment window and the monthly ceiling. Section 91 supplies the worker definition. |
| GUD-29 | 13, 54 | Sections 18 and 91 cover the relevant worker, qualifying service, deferral limits, payment during employment and the separate termination provision. |
| GUD-30 | 30–31 | Section 54 uses its own workplace scope and separates time away, pay eligibility, nursing time and protection against dismissal during specified absences. |

Both government transcriptions misnumber two headings. The printed volume labels them 5 and 7, correctly letters the worker exclusions (e) and (f), and contains no extra (c) in section 7(2)(b). The public guides use the printed numbering. None relies on the erroneous labels.

The Assembly’s scan of the 2005 trade-union amendment remains damaged. The complete eleven-section transcription includes the Clerk’s certification of 23 March and presidential assent of 30 March 2005. Its provisions operate on the Trade Unions Act. The Ministry’s printed consolidated Cap. T14, Issue 2 cites 2005 No. 17 and confirms the overlap concerning union membership and check-off dues, particularly on pp. 11 and 13. The National Industrial Court also discussed that overlap in *Ezego v NUBTE*, paragraphs 69–72. The four guides make no claim about union dues under Labour Act section 5(3) or (4). The 2004 bill was excluded as evidence of enacted wording.
